### 3 · R3754

**When Personality Meets Platform: The Moderating Role of Human-Likeness in AI User Evaluations**

2025 · International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction · article · en · https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2025.2604629

This study examines how Big Five personality traits interact with the perceived human-likeness of AI to influence user evaluations of two functionally distinct AI platforms: Replika (socio-emotional companion) and ChatGPT (task-oriented assistant). Analyzing 20,000 user reviews, we found that personality traits significantly predict user ratings. Extraversion was linked to more positive evaluations for both AI platforms. In contrast, Conscientiousness and Neuroticism predict more negative evaluations. Unexpectedly, Openness also correlated with more negative responses, challenging the assumption that open users are more receptive to emotionally rich AI. Perceived human-likeness moderated these effects differently for each platform. For Replika, it enhanced the positive effects of Extraversion and mitigated the negative impacts of Openness and Neuroticism. However, for ChatGPT, increased human-likeness only reversed the negative effects of Neuroticism, leaving the impacts of Openness and Extraversion unchanged. Implications are discussed for personality-contingent anthropomorphic design.

### 12 · R2819

**Large Language Model Automated Construction of Continuous-Adjoint Topology Optimization Solvers**

2026 · Elsevier BV · posted-content · https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7358525

Continuous-adjoint topology optimization (TO) relies on explicit adjoint equations and design sensitivities that must be re-derived when the governing equations, objective, constraints, or optimization variable change. Here we use large language models (LLMs) to generate problem-specific continuous adjoints, verification code, and complete TO code, reducing the required manual work. Three target files specify the formulation, verification, and optimization. Structural TO and fluid TO reference packages connect the formulation and derivation to adjoint-field and sensitivity checks and the TO loop. The LLM first writes a derivation containing the target residual, continuous-adjoint equations, and sensitivities. It then generates verification code comparing implemented adjoint fields with adjoints from the transposed residual Jacobian and sensitivities from automatic differentiation. Only code passing both checks is used to generate the complete TO code. Across six problems, four LLM configurations, and two reference packages, the verification gate admitted 31 of 48 combinations; all 31 produced a complete TO code passing the final checks within three Step~3 generations, and 82 of 93 Step~3 codes passed. In these problems, the conjugate heat-transfer case retained coupled velocity--pressure--temperature adjoints and sensitivities. Without a target interface-filtering structural optimization implementation, the LLM generated and checked its adjoint filter equation, sensitivities with respect to \(R_{\mathrm{eff}}\), and a complete solver with prescribed \(\gamma_{\mathrm{seed}}\) updates. By relying on reusable instructions and references and independent checks, the LLM automates continuous-adjoint TO solver construction for new formulations, from problem-specific derivation to complete TO code.
