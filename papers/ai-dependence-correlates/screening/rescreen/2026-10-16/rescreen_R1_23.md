**Prompt Coupling: Formalizing Cross-Model Prompt Dependencies in Large Language Model Systems**

2026 · Elsevier BV · posted-content, preprint · https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6262638

Large language model prompts are widely treated as portable text, yet research demonstrates that formatting alone causes accuracy variations of up to 78.3 percentage points within a single model and optimal format overlap between model families falls below an Intersection-over-Union of 0.2. We argue that existing terminology -- model drifting, prompt brittleness, prompt sensitivity -- describes symptoms of a deeper structural phenomenon we term prompt coupling: the degree of interdependence between a prompt and the specific LLM it targets. This is a conceptual and analytical paper. As multi-model deployment strategies become the enterprise norm, prompt coupling represents an unaddressed dimension of vendor lock-in with economic and regulatory implications.

**Psychological Outcomes and Correlates Associated with Over-Reliance on Artificial Intelligence Tools: An Integrative Review**

2026 · International Journal of Mental Health and Addiction · journalarticle, review, article · en · https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-026-01724-1

Abstract The growing use of artificial intelligence (AI) has raised concerns about over-reliance and its psychological implications. This integrative review synthesised psychological outcomes and correlates associated with AI over-reliance and examined its conceptualisation and measurement. The review included 28 studies identified through database and citation searching. AI over-reliance was associated with depression, anxiety, psychological distress, and cognitive disengagement, while findings concerning well-being and self-related functioning, including self-efficacy and autonomy, were mixed and context-dependent. Evidence concerning social or relational functioning was particularly limited, with only one included study directly examining this domain as an outcome. No consensus definition or measure emerged; studies examined reliance, psychological dependence, problematic or compulsive use, and addiction-like engagement. AI over-reliance is therefore best understood as an overarching, multidimensional concept. These findings highlight the need for clearer conceptual boundaries, standardised measurement, and longitudinal and experimental research. PROSPERO CRD420251038303.
